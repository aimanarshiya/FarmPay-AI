# FarmPay AI
## An AI-Powered Agricultural Digital Commerce and Settlement Platform

**Connecting Farmers, Buyers and Agricultural Markets Through Transparent Trade, Intelligent Price Discovery and Digital Commerce**

---

## 1. Project Overview

Agriculture is not only about producing crops; it is also about ensuring that the people who grow them can access fair markets, reliable buyers, transparent prices and timely payments.

Farmers frequently face difficulties when selling their produce. They may have limited access to multiple buyers, incomplete information about prevailing market prices, dependence on intermediaries, uncertain transportation arrangements and limited visibility into the actual amount they will receive after deductions.

On the other side, agricultural buyers, traders, wholesalers, processors and procurement organisations need a reliable way to discover available produce, compare offers, communicate with farmers and coordinate procurement.

**FarmPay AI is an AI-powered agricultural digital commerce and settlement platform designed to bridge this gap.**

The platform creates a digital trading environment in which farmers can publish their available produce, buyers can discover listings and submit competitive offers, and farmers can evaluate those offers based on their actual expected earnings rather than simply selecting the highest quoted price.

By combining an agricultural marketplace, explainable financial comparison, AI-assisted decision support, buyer trust information, digital order management and PayPal Sandbox payment integration, FarmPay AI demonstrates how technology can make agricultural trade more accessible, transparent and organised.

The platform is designed around three primary stakeholders:

- Farmers who want better access to buyers and transparent selling decisions.
- Buyers who want to discover agricultural produce and participate in organised procurement.
- Administrators who oversee platform activity, users and marketplace operations.

Additional experiences support collective selling, demand discovery, logistics visibility, digital crop records and dispute-management workflows.

## 2. The Problem We Are Addressing

Agricultural commerce involves several participants, including farmers, local traders, commission agents, wholesalers, processors, transport providers and procurement organisations.

However, the process of connecting these participants and completing a trade can remain fragmented.

### 2.1 Limited Direct Market Access

Many farmers depend on local market networks or intermediaries to identify buyers. Finding multiple potential buyers and comparing their offers can be difficult, particularly when the farmer does not have access to a convenient digital marketplace.

This can limit the farmer's ability to explore competitive purchasing opportunities.

### 2.2 Lack of Price Transparency

A quoted buying price does not necessarily represent the actual amount a farmer receives.

For example, two buyers may offer different prices for the same crop, but their transportation arrangements and other deductions may also differ.

Choosing an offer only by its quoted price can result in a less favourable financial outcome.

Farmers need a way to understand:

- The buyer's quoted price.
- The quantity the buyer is willing to purchase.
- The gross transaction value.
- Transportation costs and other known deductions.
- The estimated net amount payable to the farmer.
- The effective net realisation per kilogram.

### 2.3 Fragmented Buyer Discovery

Buyers such as mandi traders, wholesalers, food processors, mills and agricultural procurement organisations need access to information about available produce.

Without a centralised discovery platform, identifying suitable crops, quantities and locations can involve considerable coordination.

### 2.4 Trust and Reliability Concerns

Agricultural transactions depend on trust between farmers and buyers.

Farmers need information that helps them assess whether a buyer has a reliable transaction history. Buyers also need clear information about the available produce and the seller.

A marketplace should distinguish between verified information, recorded transaction history and information that is not yet available.

### 2.5 Transaction and Payment Coordination

Even after a buyer and farmer agree on a trade, the transaction may involve several steps:

- Offer submission.
- Offer acceptance.
- Order creation.
- Payment coordination.
- Fulfilment and delivery.
- Transaction status updates.

When these activities are handled across disconnected channels, tracking the progress of a trade becomes difficult.

### 2.6 Language and Digital Accessibility

Farmers may not always be comfortable navigating complex digital forms or typing lengthy crop descriptions.

A farmer-friendly platform should reduce the effort required to create a listing and make important information easier to understand.

## 5. Core Innovations

FarmPay AI combines three central innovations to improve the agricultural selling and procurement experience.

### 5.1 FarmPay FairBid — Intelligent Net-Realisation Comparison

**Problem:** The highest quoted price is not always the best financial offer for a farmer.

A buyer may offer a higher price but require the farmer to pay transportation costs. Another buyer may offer a slightly lower price while arranging transportation.

FarmPay FairBid is designed to compare offers using their financial implications.

The comparison considers available information such as:

- Quoted price per kilogram.
- Offered quantity.
- Gross transaction amount.
- Known transportation cost.
- Other explicitly supplied deductions.
- Estimated net proceeds.
- Net realisation per kilogram.

A simplified calculation is:

**Gross transaction value**

`Gross Amount = Offered Price × Offered Quantity`

**Estimated net proceeds**

`Net Proceeds = Gross Amount − Known Deductions`

**Net realisation per kilogram**

`Net Realisation = Net Proceeds ÷ Offered Quantity`

For example, consider a hypothetical transaction for 500 kg of produce.

| Parameter | Buyer A | Buyer B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Quoted price/kg | ₹26 | ₹25.50 |
| Quantity | 500 kg | 500 kg |
| Gross amount | ₹13,000 | ₹12,750 |
| Transport cost | ₹200 | ₹0 |
| Estimated net proceeds | ₹12,800 | ₹12,750 |
| Net realisation/kg | ₹25.60 | ₹25.50 |

Buyer A provides a slightly higher estimated net realisation in this example.

The purpose of FairBid is to help farmers understand the financial trade-offs behind competing offers.

The platform should not invent missing deductions or treat unknown costs as zero without making that assumption explicit.

**Key principle:** FarmPay FairBid supports informed decisions. The farmer remains in control of accepting or rejecting an offer.

### 5.2 Voice-to-Trade AI — Making Digital Selling Easier

**Problem:** Creating a detailed agricultural listing through a conventional form may be inconvenient for some farmers.

Voice-to-Trade introduces a voice-assisted listing experience.

The intended workflow is:

1. The farmer opens the Voice-to-Trade interface.
2. The farmer speaks the crop details.
3. The browser's supported speech-recognition capability converts speech into text.
4. The interface allows the farmer to review and edit the transcript.
5. The system assists in identifying relevant listing fields.
6. Missing information can be requested.
7. The farmer reviews the completed listing.
8. The farmer explicitly confirms before publishing.

The experience is designed with English, Telugu and Hindi language options, subject to browser and speech-recognition support.

Manual text entry remains available as an alternative.

**Key principle:** Voice assistance should simplify listing creation without publishing information without the farmer's confirmation.

### 5.3 Buyer Trust Passport — More Informed Buyer Selection

**Problem:** Farmers need more information when deciding whether to transact with a buyer.

The Buyer Trust Passport experience is designed to present buyer-related information and transaction history.

Relevant information may include:

- Buyer identity and profile details.
- Business information.
- Verification status, where actually verified.
- Completed transaction history.
- Fulfilment history.
- Recorded cancellations.
- Payment-related transaction history, where available.

New buyers or buyers without sufficient history should be clearly identified as having insufficient transaction history.

The platform should not manufacture trust scores, successful transactions or verification claims.

**Key principle:** Trust information should be based on recorded evidence rather than unsupported ratings.

## 7. Agricultural Commerce Workflow — From Farmer to Buyer

A farmer creates an account and accesses the farmer workspace.

- Available quantity.

A buyer submits an offer against a listing.

- Offered price.
- Offered quantity.

The farmer reviews the offers and compares their estimated net financial outcomes.

The platform provides payment-related functionality, including a PayPal Sandbox integration for demonstration and testing.

### Step 9 — Order and Fulfilment Visibility

The order and logistics experiences provide visibility into the transaction's progress.

This creates a connected digital journey from produce discovery to transaction coordination.

## 9. Payment Integration — PayPal Sandbox

FarmPay AI includes a PayPal REST API v2 integration in Sandbox mode.

The purpose is to demonstrate a digital payment workflow associated with an agricultural order.

The intended payment sequence is:

1. The buyer initiates the payment process.
2. The backend creates a PayPal order.
3. The buyer authorises the payment through PayPal Sandbox.
4. The backend captures the authorised payment.
5. The backend validates the capture response and transaction amount.
6. The application records the relevant payment information against the FarmPay order.
7. The payment status can be reflected in the application's transaction workflow.

### Important payment disclosure

The current integration uses PayPal Sandbox.

- It is intended for testing and demonstration.
- It does not represent live production payment processing.
- Sandbox transactions do not constitute real INR settlements.
- Production payment activation requires appropriate merchant configuration, credentials, currency support and compliance review.

FarmPay AI does not claim to operate a regulated escrow service or hold customer funds.

## 16. Installation and Local Setup

The following instructions describe the intended local development setup.

### Prerequisites

Install:

- Python 3.11 or a compatible supported Python version.
- Node.js and npm.
- MySQL 8.0.
- Git.

### Step 1 — Clone the repository

```bash
git clone https://github.com/aimanarshiya/FarmPay-AI.git
cd FarmPay-AI
```

### Step 2 — Configure MySQL

Create the application database:

```sql
CREATE DATABASE farmpay_ai
CHARACTER SET utf8mb4
COLLATE utf8mb4_unicode_ci;
```

Create a dedicated application database user and grant only the permissions required by the application.

Configure the database connection in `backend/.env`.

Review the current schema and the documented setup instructions before applying SQL scripts. Do not run privileged migration helpers or development seed scripts against an existing or production database.

### Step 3 — Set up the backend

Open a terminal in the project root:

```bash
cd backend
```

Create a Python virtual environment:

```bash
python -m venv .venv
```

Activate it on Windows PowerShell:

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\Activate.ps1
```

Install the dependencies:

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
```

Copy the backend environment template:

```powershell
Copy-Item .env.example .env
```

Configure the required values in `backend/.env`.

Important configuration variables include:

```env
PORT=8000
FRONTEND_BASE_URL=http://localhost:5173

DB_HOST=127.0.0.1
DB_PORT=3306
DB_USER=farmpay_app
DB_PASSWORD=your_db_password
DB_NAME=farmpay_ai

APP_ENV=development
JWT_SECRET=your_unique_generated_secret
JWT_ALGORITHM=HS256

GROQ_API_KEY=your_groq_api_key
GROQ_MODEL=openai/gpt-oss-20b

PAYPAL_CLIENT_ID=your_paypal_sandbox_client_id
PAYPAL_CLIENT_SECRET=your_paypal_sandbox_client_secret
PAYPAL_BASE_URL=https://api-m.sandbox.paypal.com
PAYPAL_MODE=sandbox
```

Use the actual configuration required by the current application. Optional AI-provider settings can be left empty when they are not being used.

Generate a strong JWT secret locally rather than using the example value:

```bash
python -c "import secrets; print(secrets.token_urlsafe(48))"
```

Start the backend from the `backend` directory:

```bash
python -m uvicorn app.main:app --reload
```

The local backend is expected at:

`http://127.0.0.1:8000`

FastAPI documentation, when enabled, is available at:

`http://127.0.0.1:8000/docs`

### Step 4 — Set up the frontend

Open another terminal:

```bash
cd frontend
```

Install dependencies:

```bash
npm install
```

Copy the frontend environment template:

```powershell
Copy-Item .env.example .env.local
```

Configure:

```env
VITE_API_URL=http://127.0.0.1:8000
VITE_DEMO_MODE=false
```

Start the frontend:

```bash
npm run dev
```

The local frontend is expected at:

`http://localhost:5173`

### Step 5 — Open FarmPay AI

Open the frontend URL in your browser.

Ensure that:

- MySQL is running.
- The backend starts successfully.
- The frontend starts successfully.
- The frontend can communicate with the backend.
- Required environment variables are configured.

## 18. Current Prototype Scope and Limitations

FarmPay AI is being presented as a hackathon prototype demonstrating an integrated agricultural commerce concept.

The following distinctions are important:

**Core marketplace:** Farmer, buyer, listing, offer and order workflows use the existing FastAPI and MySQL application where implemented.

**AI capabilities:** AI-assisted experiences depend on configured providers and available service access. Fallback behaviour may be used when an external provider is unavailable.

**PayPal:** The current payment integration is PayPal Sandbox and does not represent live INR settlement.

**Additional screens:** The 17-screen experience includes operational and advanced concepts that may use illustrative data or prototype interactions where backend integration has not been implemented.

**Escrow:** An escrow-themed interface is not evidence of an actual regulated escrow arrangement or custody of funds.

**Market and demand information:** Demonstration information should not be represented as verified live market data unless connected to and validated against a live source.

**Production readiness:** Production use would require additional security, payment, legal, compliance, infrastructure and field validation.

These boundaries are stated to ensure that the demonstration remains technically transparent and credible.
